cars will need to be manufactured during the next quarter in
order to meet the predicted demand. The manufacturer
assesses their current inventory and finds that they only have
enough engine parts to manufacture 2,000 cars and therefore
needs to order additional stock from their suppliers. A first
supplier is able to commit to delivering engine parts suffi-
cient for the manufacturing of 5,000 cars within a one month
time frame. A second supplier is able to commit to delivering
engine parts sufficient for the manufacturing of 3,000 cars
with a two month lead time.A third supplier is able to deliver
any requested volume of engine parts with a delivery time of
only two weeks, but charges a premium for the expedited
delivery. The automotive manufacturer uses this supplier
information to plan production scheduling such that car
production ramps up after 1.5 months to ensure that com-
ponent availability is adequate and demand is met in time.
The third supplier is used as an auxiliary supplier to mitigate
risk in the event that delays are encountered with the primary
suppliers. In this manner, the automotive manufacturer uses
supply planning to maximize production efficiency and
optimize expenses while ensuring sufficient supply to meet
anticipated demand.
